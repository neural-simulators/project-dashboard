Twins v2

Digital twins of real rooms from a few photographs per object, with open models, scored by GATOR's protocol and simulated in Isaac Sim.

Can open models turn a few photographs per object into digital twins of real rooms that a physics simulator can use?

Yes, for the three rooms tested. From 3 to 10 photographs per object, SAM 3D Objects and GATOR's placement step give twins whose position and orientation are more accurate than GATOR's (pose error 0.035 against 0.041); their shape is still less accurate (shape error 0.056 against 0.031). After two physics steps of our own, grounding and a layout step, 50 of the 51 twins stand when every twin of a room is released together in Isaac Sim, and they respond to a push, a throw or a slide like furniture. The final pipeline uses no agent.

1. How we got here↑ contents

The figure reads left to right, top row first. The top row is about each object on its own. GATOR's route with the open Pixal3D weights placed objects well but produced chairs in fragments, which fall over in the simulator (11 of 51 stand); the repair agent GATOR uses (here Claude editing the mesh in Blender) could not mend fragments. Mundo's box proxies always stand but have no shape, so only one idea survived: a twin that falls on its own gets its bounding box as collider. Switching the generator to SAM 3D Objects was the turning point: whole meshes, the best pose error in the comparison, and 45 of 51 twins stand alone. The agent on SAM 3D meshes kept only edits that extend legs to the floor.

The bottom row is about whole rooms. Released together as placed, the twins of a room overlap each other by a few centimetres and PhysX shoots them apart. A layout step that lets PhysX push them apart slowly helped, but it keeps only sideways moves, and most overlaps were stacked: legless tables had been lowered onto the chairs beneath them. Grounding fixed the cause: every twin keeps the height its placement estimated, and missing legs are added down to the floor (the agent's fix, now automatic and free). With a sideways nudge for the overlaps left, 50 of 51 twins stand when their room settles together.

2. The final pipeline↑ contents

Blue: the reconstruction of each object from its photographs. Amber: the physics that turns a room of placed meshes into a room that simulates.

Reconstruction, per object (blue)

  1. Photographs. 3 to 10 of the room capture's DSLR photographs that show the object: what anyone with a camera has.
  2. Masks. SAM 2.1 cuts the object out of each photograph, prompted with the box and a few points of the dataset's annotation, as GATOR does.
  3. Cameras. The capture's own poses. With poses VGGT estimates from the photographs alone, the pose error only rises from 0.035 to 0.037.
  4. SAM 3D Objects. Each masked photograph becomes a coloured mesh with a pose in that photograph's camera, so one object has several candidates.
  5. Best candidate. The mesh whose outline agrees best with the masks in all photographs is kept.
  6. Placement. GATOR's step shifts and scales the mesh, and turns it slightly, until its outlines match the masks and its surface matches VGGT's 3D points.

Physics, per room (amber)

  1. Collider. CoACD splits each mesh into up to 32 convex pieces, the shapes a physics engine can collide; the mass comes from the category.
  2. Grounding. Every twin keeps the height its placement estimated. Where the generator left out legs, four grey legs (chairs, tables) or a plinth (cabinets, shelves, bins) reach down to the floor; a mesh that pokes into the floor is cut at it; an object on furniture, such as a monitor on a desk, rests on that twin.
  3. Alone in Isaac. Each twin on its own on a floor for 3 s: 49 of 51 stand. One that tips over gets its bounding box as collider instead (Mundo's rule).
  4. Layout. Twins placed one by one can overlap their neighbours by a few centimetres. A short PhysX run with gravity off pushes them apart slowly, then the lighter twin of any pair still overlapping is moved sideways by the smallest step that clears it, so the room starts with no overlap.
  5. Room in Isaac. Every twin of the room together on the floor, then a push, a throw or a slide.
Where ground truth enters. The annotation's boxes and points prompt the masks (as in GATOR), the capture's camera poses are used in the main condition, and the floor plane sits at the laser scan's floor height. In pictures and clips the scan is only the backdrop: it is not a collider and walls are not simulated. The scanned objects are used only to score.
Where an agent is used. Nowhere in the final pipeline. The only agent tried is GATOR's Blender repair agent (Claude Sonnet): 2 of 14 repairs kept on Pixal3D meshes, 3 of 14 on SAM 3D meshes, about $5 of API use; every kept repair extended legs to the floor, which grounding now does for every twin without a model call (appendix B).

D. Rooms before grounding (retired)

Released as placed, then with the layout step alone; why both grounding and the layout step are needed.

The physics of whole rooms before grounding. Released as placed, every twin snapped to the floor on its own: legless tables and shelves were lowered by up to half a metre onto the chairs beneath them, a table whose mesh reached below the floor was raised by 48 cm, and a monitor and a notebook went from the desk to the floor, into the desk. PhysX then shot the overlapping twins apart in the first 0.1 s. The layout step (a gentle PhysX push-apart) helped but keeps only sideways moves, so stacked overlaps came back. Grounding without the layout step fixes the office, where the errors were supports, but the conference rooms still explode; both halves are needed.

Every measure, defined
ADD-SB
Chamfer distance between the posed twin and the scanned object, divided by the object's diameter, with no alignment: shape and placement together. ADD-SB < 0.1 is the share below 0.1.
CD, NC, F1
After a similarity ICP: mean two-way nearest-neighbour distance over the diameter; mean absolute normal agreement; harmonic mean of precision and recall at 0.02 D.
IoU2D
Outline IoU of the posed twin against the masks, averaged over the object's photographs.
Diameter D
Convex-hull diameter of the scanned object (ScanNet++) or of the artist model (HANDAL).
Stands
Tilt of the up axis below 10°, horizontal drift below 5 cm and no sinking by more than 10 cm (3 s alone, or 4 s with the whole room).
