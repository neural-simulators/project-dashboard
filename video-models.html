Video models

Open video diffusion models continuing the physics test tasks from one frame and a sentence, against the physics engine.

Can an off-the-shelf video diffusion model continue a physics scene from one frame and a sentence?

The learned simulators on the other tabs predict object states. Here a representative subset of the same frozen test tasks is given to four open video diffusion models (the open models of the Principia benchmark, Thozhiyoor et al. 2026): each gets the first rendered frame of the PhysX rollout and a text prompt, and must produce the next 5 seconds of video. The physics engine's own render of the rollout is the ground truth, and the models are scored in image space: the task's objects are tracked in every video with SAM 2 and compared with the exact projected PhysX motion. This is a first look at how such models fail on our scenes, not a benchmark run: the subset is small (the 14B models take 8 to 20 minutes per 5 s clip) and the numbers carry wide error bars.

Two prompts per task: the action prompt says only what is done at t = 0 (the information a learned simulator receives), the outcome prompt adds what actually happens in the rollout. Where the co-author's V5 learned simulator was rendered on the same clips, it is shown and scored alongside, by the same tracker.

How videos are scored↑ contents

Nothing is lifted to 3D. The task's objects (the acted object and the objects named by the task) are tracked in every video with SAM 2.1 (video predictor, large), seeded at frame 0 with each object's exact box: the PhysX state is projected into the picture with the render camera's pinhole model (18 mm focal length, 20.955 mm aperture, 480 x 360, verified against the renders). The same projection gives the PhysX centre of every object in every later frame, so the errors are distances in the picture between the tracked mask centroid and the projected centre, over the 3 s of the rollout, at the video's own frame times. Errors are given as a share of the frame diagonal and, converted at the object's true depth, in metres (the component perpendicular to the viewing ray; depth along the ray is not observable from one fixed camera and is not scored). Masks also give the amount, pace and direction of motion, whether other task objects move when they should, and whether an object vanishes, leaves the picture or balloons. The same tracker on the physics engine's own render shows what a perfect video would score (the floor of every error). Each model's video is first resized to 480 x 360, so the models' different output sizes do not enter the numbers.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • No video model keeps the pace of the physics engine. Median time scale with the outcome prompt: Wan 14B 0.45, Wan 5B 0.14, Cosmos 14B 0.10, Cosmos 2B 0.06 (1 = the engine's pace; with the action prompt 0.0 to 0.18). Our V5 predictors score 0.53 (regression) and 0.79 (flow matching) on the clips they were rendered for, the tracker on the truth itself 0.99.
  • Wan 14B with the outcome prompt is the only model in the same league as our state predictors, and it is still far off.   • Frozen: the acted object never sets off although the prompt names the action (and, with the outcome prompt, the result): 10 to 55% of the videos depending on model and prompt.
  • Too slow: when objects do move, they move at a fraction of the true pace, so the contact the task is about arrives late or never.
  • Wrong direction: thrown and pushed objects leave in a direction unrelated to the prompt's (cosine 0.1 to 0.3 for three of the four models).

How to read the tables↑ contents

Every measure, defined
Actor error
average distance between the tracked centre of the acted object in the video and the exact projected PhysX centre, over the 3 s of the rollout, as a share of the frame diagonal (480 x 360 px, D = 600 px; lower is better). The same tracker on the physics engine's own render gives the floor.
Final error
the same distance at 3 s.
Actor error (m), final error (m)
the same pixel errors converted to metres at the object's true depth (pixel error x depth / focal length, f = 412 px): the component of the 3D position error perpendicular to the viewing ray, comparable to the position errors of the learned simulators on the other tabs. The component along the viewing ray is not observable from one fixed camera and is not measured.
Time scale
how fast the acted object moves along its PhysX path: every video frame is matched to the PhysX time whose position is nearest (monotonically), and the slope of matched PhysX time against video time is fitted while PhysX still moves. 1 = the right pace; 0.3 = three times too slow; 0 = it never sets off.
Motion ratio
length of the acted object's tracked path divided by the length of its PhysX path over 3 s (1 = the right amount of motion).
Direction
average cosine between the tracked and the PhysX displacement over 0.2 s windows in which PhysX moves the object by more than 1% of D (1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated, below 0 = opposite).
Knock-ons
of the other task objects that PhysX moves by more than 3% of D, the share that the video moves by at least half as much.
Frozen
share of videos in which the acted object moves less than 2% of D while PhysX moves it more than 8% of D.
Vanished / left frame / deformed
share of videos in which a task object's mask at 3 s is smaller than 20% of its mask at 0 s while the object was last seen well inside the picture (vanished: it disappeared, merged or the tracker lost it), or near the picture's edge or where PhysX also puts it outside (left the frame); and larger than 250% (deformed, duplicated or ballooned).
Settling
speed of the acted object in the video after 3 s, when every PhysX object is at rest (share of D per second; 0 is right).
SSIM
structural similarity between the video frame and the physics engine's render at 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 s (480 x 360); 1 = identical. Only where the renders exist (PNG frames).
Reviewer
a vision-language model (Qwen3.8-27B, the same reviewer as on the Improved Results V5 tab) sees the task instruction and five frames (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 s) and answers whether the task succeeded, the motion is plausible and every object kept its identity; the same questions on the physics engine's render calibrate it.
