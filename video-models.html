Video models

Open video diffusion models continuing the physics test tasks from one frame and a sentence, against the physics engine.

Can an off-the-shelf video diffusion model predict the physics of a held-out test scene from one frame and a sentence?

41 held-out test records of our final dataset (28 from the final batch, 8 from the simple set, 5 from Dataset V2), one per motion family; no scene among them was seen in training. Four open video models (Wan2.2 TI2V-5B, Wan2.2 I2V-A14B, Cosmos-Predict2.5 2B and Cosmos-Predict2.5 14B) each get the first frame of the physics engine's render and a sentence, in two prompts: the outcome prompt says what happens, the action prompt only what starts the motion. Our four 200M state predictors get the 3D scene and the action program instead and are scored the same way: the followed object is tracked in every clip and compared with the physics engine (PhysX) over the first 5 s.

The earlier 20-item round with the older V5 model is in the closed block at the bottom of the page.

Video models against our state predictors on 41 test records↑ contents

The current evaluation (November 2026). 41 records from the held-out test scenes of Dataset V2, one per motion family, each the hardest one the camera shows clearly (33 furnished rooms, 8 without furniture). Four video models (Wan2.2 TI2V-5B, Wan2.2 I2V-A14B, Cosmos-Predict2.5 2B and Cosmos-Predict2.5 14B) each get frame 0 of the physics engine's render and a sentence, in two versions: the outcome prompt says what happens, the action prompt only what starts the motion. Our four 200M state predictors get the 3D scene and the action program. Everything is scored on the first 5 s against the physics engine (PhysX). 36 records are scorable: on the other 5 the tracker cannot follow even the physics engine's own render.

Position error: image-plane distance of the followed object from the physics engine, in metres at the object's depth, averaged over 0 to 5 s (lower is better). Pace: 1 = the right speed. Direction: 1 = the same direction. Error, pace and path are medians over records, direction and frozen are means. Ours get the 3D scene and the action program, the video models one frame and a sentence.

  • Our state predictors are 2.5 to 7 times closer. Median position error 0.11 to 0.22 m against 0.54 to 0.73 m for the video models; regression beats every video-model clip on 34 of 36 records.
  • The video models move objects too slowly and only loosely in the right direction. Pace 0.20 to 0.68 and direction 0.07 to 0.38, against 0.85 to 0.98 and 0.70 to 0.87 for ours.
  • Saying what happens helps but does not close the gap. The outcome prompt raises every video model's pace; the best video model is still 5 times further off than our regression.

How videos are scored↑ contents

Nothing is lifted to 3D. The task's objects (the acted object and the objects named by the task) are tracked in every video with SAM 2.1 (video predictor, large), seeded at frame 0 with each object's exact box: the PhysX state is projected into the picture with the render camera's pinhole model (18 mm focal length, 20.955 mm aperture, 480 x 360, verified against the renders). The same projection gives the PhysX centre of every object in every later frame, so the errors are distances in the picture between the tracked mask centroid and the projected centre, over the 3 s of the rollout, at the video's own frame times. Errors are given as a share of the frame diagonal and, converted at the object's true depth, in metres (the component perpendicular to the viewing ray; depth along the ray is not observable from one fixed camera and is not scored). Masks also give the amount, pace and direction of motion, whether other task objects move when they should, and whether an object vanishes, leaves the picture or balloons. The same tracker on the physics engine's own render shows what a perfect video would score (the floor of every error). Each model's video is first resized to 480 x 360, so the models' different output sizes do not enter the numbers.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • No video model keeps the pace of the physics engine. Median time scale with the outcome prompt: Wan 14B 0.45, Wan 5B 0.14, Cosmos 14B 0.10, Cosmos 2B 0.06 (1 = the engine's pace; with the action prompt 0.0 to 0.18). Our V5 predictors score 0.53 (regression) and 0.79 (flow matching) on the clips they were rendered for, the tracker on the truth itself 0.99.
  • Wan 14B with the outcome prompt is the only model in the same league as our state predictors, and it is still far off. Pulled-object error at depth 0.67 m, direction cosine 0.59, reviewer 'task achieved' 35% (the reviewer grants 38% to the truth), against 0.9 to 1.15 m, cosine 0.08 to 0.33 and 20 to 30% for the other three models; V5 regression: 0.15 m, cosine 0.76 on its five clips; tracker floor 0.12 m.
  • Telling the model what happens helps every model and settles the prompt question. Averaged over the four models the outcome prompt lowers the error from 1.10 to 0.92 m, doubles the pace (0.09 to 0.19), raises the direction cosine from 0.22 to 0.32, lowers the frozen share from 38% to 25% and lifts the reviewer's success rate from 6% to 26%. The gallery therefore shows the outcome-prompt clips; the action-prompt clips are in the fold below them.
  • Each family fails in its own way. Wan 5B morphs and invents (reviewer: something looks wrong in 60 to 80% of its clips; deformation 30% with the outcome prompt). Cosmos 2B barely moves anything (45% frozen with the action prompt, time scale 0.06, direction 0.08) and never moves the other task objects unless told. Cosmos 14B keeps objects intact best (reviewer identity 75 to 90%) but at a fifth of the pace, deforms a quarter of the time and takes 20 minutes per clip. Wan 14B moves at the right scale more often but loses a task object out of the picture in a quarter to a third of its videos.
  • Small, slow motions look fine; big, fast ones do not. Pendulums, springs and the soft body sit within 0.1 to 0.2 m of the truth for every model because the acted object barely travels; throws, slides, dominoes, topples and Jenga pulls are 1 to 2.4 m off, with the acted object frozen in up to half of the videos.
  • Cost. One 5 s clip takes 2 to 20 minutes on one GPU (Wan 5B to Cosmos 14B); our 23 M-parameter predictor rolls out 3 s in 1.2 s alone or 30 ms batched on the same GPU.

How each model behaves↑ contents

Wan2.2 TI2V-5B

  • Outcome prompt: error 0.89 m, time scale 0.14, direction 0.27, 35% frozen, 30% deformed; action prompt: 1.08 m, time scale 0.00, 55% frozen. The reviewer finds something physically wrong in 60 to 80% of its clips and broken identity in 60 to 75%: it invents a second bucket, turns a pendulum bob into a cabinet, bends dominoes into a strip.
  • Fastest model (2 to 6 min per clip) and the least usable.

Wan2.2 I2V-A14B

  • Best video model here once told the outcome: 0.67 m, time scale 0.45, direction 0.59, reviewer success 35% (= what the reviewer grants the physics engine); best on drops, hits, ramps, rebounds, Jenga and the room scenes. With the action prompt it drops to 1.07 m and 0.18 (35% frozen).
  • Its typical errors: a task object leaves the picture (25 to 35% of videos), things slide or fall before anything reaches them, strings become rods.

Cosmos-Predict2.5 2B

  • Most faithful picture (SSIM 0.93) and the least motion: 45% frozen and knock-ons 0% with the action prompt; with the outcome prompt the acted object moves but at time scale 0.06 and in an unrelated direction (cosine 0.08). Reviewer: 65 to 70% plausible, 60 to 65% identity.
  • Its guardrail rejected the words 'domino' and 'flat' and distances like '2.3 m'; all prompts were rewritten for all models.

Cosmos-Predict2.5 14B

  • Keeps object identity best (reviewer 75 to 90% identity, 70 to 90% plausible) and freezes least (10 to 15%), but moves at a fifth to a tenth of the true pace (time scale 0.10 to 0.16) with errors of 0.95 to 1.10 m and deformation in a quarter to a third of the clips; best of the four on throws. 20 minutes per clip on an A100 or RTX PRO 6000, out of memory on a 48 GB L40S.

Failure patterns across models

  • Frozen: the acted object never sets off although the prompt names the action (and, with the outcome prompt, the result): 10 to 55% of the videos depending on model and prompt.
  • Too slow: when objects do move, they move at a fraction of the true pace, so the contact the task is about arrives late or never.
  • Wrong direction: thrown and pushed objects leave in a direction unrelated to the prompt's (cosine 0.1 to 0.3 for three of the four models).
  • Identity: objects change shape, split, duplicate or dissolve, most often the small thin ones (dominoes, pendulum bobs, strings) and most often with the longer outcome prompt for Wan 5B.
  • Effects without causes: dominoes fall and blocks slide before anything touches them, mainly in Wan 14B's videos; and objects leave the picture (Wan models).

How to read the tables↑ contents

Every measure, defined
Actor error
average distance between the tracked centre of the acted object in the video and the exact projected PhysX centre, over the 3 s of the rollout, as a share of the frame diagonal (480 x 360 px, D = 600 px; lower is better). The same tracker on the physics engine's own render gives the floor.
Final error
the same distance at 3 s.
Actor error (m), final error (m)
the same pixel errors converted to metres at the object's true depth (pixel error x depth / focal length, f = 412 px): the component of the 3D position error perpendicular to the viewing ray, comparable to the position errors of the learned simulators on the other tabs. The component along the viewing ray is not observable from one fixed camera and is not measured.
Time scale
how fast the acted object moves along its PhysX path: every video frame is matched to the PhysX time whose position is nearest (monotonically), and the slope of matched PhysX time against video time is fitted while PhysX still moves. 1 = the right pace; 0.3 = three times too slow; 0 = it never sets off.
Motion ratio
length of the acted object's tracked path divided by the length of its PhysX path over 3 s (1 = the right amount of motion).
Direction
average cosine between the tracked and the PhysX displacement over 0.2 s windows in which PhysX moves the object by more than 1% of D (1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated, below 0 = opposite).
Knock-ons
of the other task objects that PhysX moves by more than 3% of D, the share that the video moves by at least half as much.
Frozen
share of videos in which the acted object moves less than 2% of D while PhysX moves it more than 8% of D.
Vanished / left frame / deformed
share of videos in which a task object's mask at 3 s is smaller than 20% of its mask at 0 s while the object was last seen well inside the picture (vanished: it disappeared, merged or the tracker lost it), or near the picture's edge or where PhysX also puts it outside (left the frame); and larger than 250% (deformed, duplicated or ballooned).
Settling
speed of the acted object in the video after 3 s, when every PhysX object is at rest (share of D per second; 0 is right).
SSIM
structural similarity between the video frame and the physics engine's render at 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 s (480 x 360); 1 = identical. Only where the renders exist (PNG frames).
Reviewer
a vision-language model (Qwen3.8-27B, the same reviewer as on the Improved Results V5 tab) sees the task instruction and five frames (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 s) and answers whether the task succeeded, the motion is plausible and every object kept its identity; the same questions on the physics engine's render calibrate it.
