Improved Results V7

Iteration 7: two ways to get multi-step behaviour into flow matching without a loss through its sampler, each checked against its control after a few hours of training; and every method so far compared on the same clips.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

In short↑ contents

  • Tried: (1) flow matching on top of the multi-step regression model, so flow only models the spread around regression; (2) engine continuations: the engine's correct next 0.2 s from flow's own drifted states, added to flow's training.
  • (1) did not help: test 0.395 to 0.392 m, but validation 0.368 to 0.378 m, and worse with more training (0.399 m). Flow learns no spread (sampled and noise-free agree to 0.001 m): the inputs decide the next 0.2 s, and the one-block training undoes the chain training.
  • (2) made flow worse: 0.494 to 0.502 m, bystanders moved 4.4% to 8.4%. The correct continuation from a drifted state keeps the drift.
  • Both stopped at the 6-hour check. Below: every method compared visually. All of last week on one page: the Ablations tab.

What changed (25 Oct 2026, both stopped at the 6-hour check: neither helps)↑ contents

Multi-step (chained) training lowers regression's error but not flow matching's (iteration 6): the engine's block k is the wrong target once a rollout has drifted, and the loss reaches flow only through its sampler. Two ways around it, both with exact targets and no loss through the sampler: (1) keep the multi-step regression model and let flow model only what it misses; (2) give flow the physically correct next 0.2 s from the states its own rollouts reach, from the physics engine.

VariantWhat it adds
Flow on top of multi-step regressionthe best multi-step regression model (exact geometry, 5-block chains) is frozen; flow matching learns the engine's block minus the regression block, one block at a time from the engine's states. It starts from the regression weights with its input and output layers at zero, so at step 0 it is exactly the regression model; the regression model also decides which objects stay still. Three noise scales of the residual (1, 0.3, 0.1).
Engine continuations from flow's own statesthe 40,000-step flow model rolls out 8,000 training simulations; at 3 random block starts each, the physics engine continues 0.2 s from the model's own (drifted) state (24,000 starts on 356 training scenes; starts where the model left objects deep inside each other are rejected). Flow is fine-tuned on the training data plus these blocks (x3), with the same recipe, steps and start as the one-step control.

Same training, validation and test simulations as iteration 6; test scenes are never used for training.

Every method on the same clips: what looks physical↑ contents

Every method so far on the 18 test and 15 training comparison scenes, judged from the clips and from each object's height and travel against the physics engine. Gravity in free fall is right for every model except block diffusion. The 50M exact-geometry model with 5-block chains and the rules looks the most physical; the 23M exact-geometry model is next. The rules fix hovering and untouched objects moving, but the contact rule makes objects in contact hop every 0.2 s and, with flow matching, can throw a ball upwards. Neither V7 method looks better than its starting model. Every method misses the trampoline bounce, the bucket toppling, dominoes beyond the first and a ball's deflection after impact.

Flow matching on top of multi-step regression↑ contents

Flow learns only the engine's block minus the frozen regression block. At step 0 the combined model is exactly the regression model (checked: identical rollouts).

Takeaways↑ contents

  • Keep multi-step regression as the forward model: flow on top of it adds no reliable accuracy, and trained longer it undoes the chain training.
  • Exact targets from the model's own drifted states make flow matching worse: what lowers the error over 3 s is steering back towards the true path, which only chained training teaches.
  • Given the full scene table the forward physics is deterministic, so flow learns almost no spread; its use is where the inputs do not decide the outcome (hidden masses or pins, backward questions), not the forward error.
  • The 50M-parameter model of iteration 6 (exact geometry, 5-block chains, rules) is the best forward model so far: 0.371 m.

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): it counted objects resting on the floor, or on anything whose contact the model missed, as in free flight, so every model and the physics engine itself score about 8-10. Ignore this column; the free-fall error replaces it.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
Free-fall error
how far the predicted vertical acceleration, measured over 0.1 s, is from gravity on stretches where the physics engine's own motion is a free fall (its vertical acceleration within 0.5 m/s² of gravity for 7 frames in a row); m/s². The physics engine itself scores 0.02, and 2 mm of position jitter adds about 0.4.
