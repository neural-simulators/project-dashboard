Improved Results V6

Iteration 6: why V5 misses collisions, generation rules that halve passing through, 40,000-step and multi-step (unrolled) training that lower the error, why multi-step training does not work for flow matching, and exact convex geometry; every model on the same test simulations and clips.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

In short↑ contents

  • Best so far: exact convex geometry, trained from scratch, then on 5-block chains (the gradient through 1 s of its own rollout), with the generation rules: test error 0.443 to 0.382 m, passing through 4.2 to 1.7 cm, objects moving before anything touches them 7.1% to 3.8%, topples caught 63% (V5: 51%).
  • Generation rules at generation time (exact floor, no overlap between exact shapes with momentum shared, objects at rest stay at rest until something reaches them, exact free flight and pendulum motion) halve passing through and bystanders moved for every model, at no cost in error (V5: 0.443 to 0.442 m).
  • Longer training (40,000 more steps, impact steps oversampled) lowers the error for both models: regression 0.443 to 0.426 m, flow matching 0.581 to 0.492 m.
  • Multi-step training works for regression (validation 0.386 to 0.348 m) but not for flow matching: 19 variants all end above flow's one-step control. Pushing one sample towards the engine's trajectory conflicts with flow's objective (a distribution); it damages the denoiser at low and middle noise (+57% to +106%). One variant beat the control for 500 steps (0.429 against 0.442).
  • Exact convex geometry (face planes of every convex part, mesh objects as their collision pieces) reads PhysX contacts much better (false overlaps 12.5% to 2.5%) and, trained from scratch, beats an identical box-geometry model at every stage (one-step 0.444 to 0.432 m, 5-block chains with the rules 0.393 to 0.382 m, passing through 1.9 to 1.7 cm). Fine-tuning a box-trained model on it failed: its inputs changed meaning.

What changed (23-24 Oct 2026, larger models (50M, 100M parameters) training)↑ contents

This iteration starts from the clips: V5 misses the push at impacts and lets objects pass through each other. First the cause (diagnosis section): from the physics engine's exact state just before an impact, V5 still gives the struck object almost none of its push; trained on many more impacts of one scene, the same network does. Then four kinds of change, each measured on the same 15,000 test simulations and the same clips: generation rules applied to every model's output, longer training with impact steps oversampled, multi-step (unrolled) training with the gradient through several chained 0.2 s blocks, and exact convex geometry in the object encoding.

VariantWhat it adds
Generation rulesapplied after every 0.2 s block at generation, to any model: exact floor (lowest point of the exact parts); no overlap between exact shapes beyond the start state's, the separation and an approaching pair's momentum shared by mass (balls bounce by restitution); an object that has not moved stays at rest until a moving object with a cause reaches it; where nothing can be touched, the built-in free-flight and pendulum motion as is
40,000 more stepsV5 trained 40,000 more steps on the training set plus 13,851 simulations of the 15 shown scenes, 30% of the steps drawn where two movable objects start touching
Multi-step (unrolled) trainingchains of 3 or 5 blocks: each block starts from the model's own previous block, the loss is the sum over the chain against the engine, the gradient flows through the whole chain; 8,000 steps from the 40,000-step model
Exact geometryup to 16 parts per object, the face planes and hull vertices of every convex part, mesh objects as their convex collision pieces, 64 surface points; gaps, contact directions and times to impact computed on them; trained from scratch next to an identical box-geometry model

All with the floor constraint at generation. Training scenes and the comparison clips are the same as in Scaled results 2.1b; test scenes are never used for training.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • Keep: the generation rules (half the passing through and bystanders, no cost in error) and multi-step training for regression (test error 0.443 to 0.393-0.407 m).
  • Longer training helps both models; flow matching gains the most from it (0.581 to 0.492 m).
  • Multi-step training as done for regression does not transfer to flow matching: a per-sample position loss through a sampler fights flow's distribution objective. A distribution-level loss on its own rollouts (as in Self Forcing) or flow on top of the multi-step regression model is the way to give flow the same gain.
  • Exact convex geometry wins when trained from scratch (0.382 m with chains and rules, the best model). The 50M and 100M parameter models with exact geometry are training; they decide the recipe for the dense impact data.

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): it counted objects resting on the floor, or on anything whose contact the model missed, as in free flight, so every model and the physics engine itself score about 8-10. Ignore this column; the free-fall error replaces it.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
Free-fall error
how far the predicted vertical acceleration, measured over 0.1 s, is from gravity on stretches where the physics engine's own motion is a free fall (its vertical acceleration within 0.5 m/s² of gravity for 7 frames in a row); m/s². The physics engine itself scores 0.02, and 2 mm of position jitter adds about 0.4.
