Improved Results V6

Iteration 6: a contact constraint at generation halves passing through and lowers the error on the whole test set; and the reason V5 misses the push at impacts: it has not seen enough of them (more impacts of a scene fix it, stronger losses and shorter steps do not).

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

In short↑ contents

  • Better on the whole test set (15,000 simulations): a contact constraint at generation (no overlap between the exact part shapes; colliding objects share their momentum, never gain any) lowers the position error (regression 0.443 to 0.434 m, flow matching 0.581 to 0.555 m), halves passing through (4.2 to 1.9 cm; simulations with a pair more than 5 cm too deep 28% to 10%) and agrees with the engine's verdict a little more often (90.2% to 90.7%). In the test clips the overlap falls from 5.6 to 3.1 cm and 29 knock-ons are missed instead of 34.
  • Why V5 fails at collisions: even when it starts from the physics engine's exact state just before an impact, V5 gives the struck object almost none of its push (median 0.00 of the true change of velocity on the shown training clips). In the drop clip the ball also slows down before it reaches the pin. The impact itself is not learned; errors earlier in the rollout only add to it.
  • It can be learned: the same network, trained further on 944 simulations of the drop scene, throws the pin 1.19 m in the first second (engine 1.23 m, V5 0.17 m) and overlaps it 3.5 cm instead of 8 cm, for an action that was not in its training data. V5 saw each 0.2 s training step about 5 times; this model saw the drop scene's steps about 100 times. The bottleneck is how often the model sees impacts, not the losses or the network.
  • What did not fix it (each from V5, on the same data): stronger contact losses, training on the model's own rollouts and weighting impacts (higher error, no gain in the clips); oversampling impact steps (no gain); 0.1 s steps (half the push with impact oversampling, error 0.499 m); one-frame steps (struck objects pushed in the training clips, fewer knock-ons missed, error 0.613 m).
  • Running now: V5 trained 40,000 more steps (8 times its budget on this data) with impact steps oversampled, regression and flow matching. Next: many more impact simulations across all training scenes, since the test scenes need impacts of every kind, not only those of the 15 shown scenes.

What changed (23-24 Oct 2026, diagnosis done; longer training running)↑ contents

Diagnosis first. Each comparison clip was rerun with the step that contains the first impact started from the physics engine's exact state, every channel or one at a time (position, rotation, velocity, spin). The acted object's response then becomes right (median 1.03), but the struck object still gets almost no push (median 0.00 to 0.15 for every regression model). An earlier check, reported on 23 Oct as 93% of the push from the true state, restarted every step from the engine and so handed the struck object its true motion from the next step on; it was wrong. Then each idea was tested from V5 on the same data (the training set plus 13,851 new simulations of the 15 shown training scenes): the losses (penetration counted only between objects within 2 cm, contact law, momentum at impacts), training on the model's own rollouts with a curriculum (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 14 own steps), weighting or oversampling the steps where two movable objects start touching, and shorter steps (0.1 s, one frame). Finally, a check of what the network can learn: V5 trained only on 944 simulations of the drop scene.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • The contact constraint at generation is the change to keep: lower error (0.443 to 0.434 m regression, 0.581 to 0.555 m flow matching), half the passing through (4.2 to 1.9 cm) and slightly more verdicts right (90.7%) on all 15,000 test simulations.
  • The impact is the learned model's failure: from the engine's exact state just before it, V5 gives the struck object almost none of its push; the ball also brakes before it reaches the pin, because the impact falls on a step boundary.
  • The network can learn it when it sees the impact often enough (drop scene: 0.93 of the push, 3.5 cm overlap, on an unseen action); V5's 20,000 steps showed each training step about 5 times.
  • Losses, own-rollout training, impact weighting and shorter steps raise the test error and do not fix the clips; one-frame steps help the struck object but cost 38% more error. The learned fix is more impacts seen in training: the 40,000-step runs (running) and impact simulations across all training scenes (next).

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): it counted objects resting on the floor, or on anything whose contact the model missed, as in free flight, so every model and the physics engine itself score about 8-10. Ignore this column; the free-fall error replaces it.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
Free-fall error
how far the predicted vertical acceleration, measured over 0.1 s, is from gravity on stretches where the physics engine's own motion is a free fall (its vertical acceleration within 0.5 m/s² of gravity for 7 frames in a row); m/s². The physics engine itself scores 0.02, and 2 mm of position jitter adds about 0.4.
