State predictors on V2

Our state predictors, trained only on the simple set, run on Dataset V2's furnished rooms they have never seen. A first qualitative look; updated 07 November 2026, 08:05 UTC.

In short

  1. Furnished rooms cost about 2.5 times the error. regression 100M, chain of 10: 0.16 m on the simple set's test rooms, 0.40 m on V2, per clip.
  2. Invented motion rises too: 19.0% of still objects moved on the simple set, 31.5% on V2.
  3. Hardest on V2: orbital (1.94 m), rocket (1.37 m), coupled springs (1.22 m), balloon (1.03 m). Easiest: books ball catch (0.04 m), trampoline (0.04 m), crate drop table (0.05 m), diving board (0.07 m).
  4. The models never saw furniture or these rooms in training; this is how far they carry over, before any training on V2.

Action evaluation · given a simulator, does the planner find an action that works in PhysX?
  1. With PhysX in the loop, Qwen reaches the outcome on 63 and 59 of 90 tasks (two runs). With no simulator, on 13 of 90. So retries with a true simulator work, and they set the ceiling.
  2. Reached in both the predictor and PhysX: non-block flow 25 of 90, non-block regression 23, masked regression (all questions) 16, masked flow (all questions) 12.
  3. Predictors often say the outcome was met when PhysX disagrees: masked flow on 35 of its 47 claims, non-block flow on 36 of 61, masked regression on 28 of 44, non-block regression on 17 of 40.
  4. The two PhysX runs differ by 4 tasks, so differences of a few tasks here are not meaningful. PhysX replays differ by more than 2 cm from the recording on 29 of 102 V2 scenes, so a clip here may not look exactly like the original clip of that scene. Tasks and verdicts all use replays.

How this was done · the task, the loop and how success is decided
  • Task: the scene of a clip on this tab and an outcome its own action misses, for example "objects toppled exactly 6". The outcome comes from simulating eight values of the scene's main action in PhysX, so the working values are known.
  • Loop: the planner (Qwen with the SFT and DPO stages) proposes an action. A simulator reports the measured outcome. The planner tries again, up to 20 times, and stops when the simulator says the outcome was met. If it never does, the attempt the simulator put closest is the answer.
  • Success check: one fixed rule on object poses, the same for PhysX and for predicted motion: distance from start to end, highest point, tilt past 45°, inside the container at the end, largest rotation, or swings. Hits are not included yet, because predicted motion has no contact reports.
  • Truth: every final action is replayed in PhysX, which decides success. So a predictor can say "met" and be wrong.

How this was done · models, data and what the numbers mean
itemvalue
modelstrained only on the simple set (no furniture); no training or fine-tuning on V2. The clips show non-block regression (still weight 0.1, 1 and 3), flow and diffusion; masked regression and masked flow (forward only and all questions) and the first masked flow runs; regression 100M at 1, 3, 5, 10 and 15 blocks; regression 25M at 1 and 15; flow 100M at 1, 3, 5 and 10; flow 50M and 25M at 1, 5 and 10
dataDataset V2 (30k + 40k runs, 67,588 kept records, filter v5), test rooms only; 1,533 sampled records, up to 30 per family and every kind of simulation represented. The 1,533 numbers come from the two regression chain models; the other models run only on the 106 clips
inputthe same as for the simple set: 60 frames per second, the robot tool removed, its force and timing acting on the object. V2 scenes hold up to 82 objects against 64 in training
errormean position error of the objects that move, averaged per clip, then over clips (the simple set's column is the same on its test rooms). The State predictors tab counts each moving object once, so its numbers differ
false motionof the objects that stay still in the simulation, the share the model moves more than 5 cm
not includedspring has no qualifying V2 test record in this sample. This is a qualitative look, not a full V2 evaluation
