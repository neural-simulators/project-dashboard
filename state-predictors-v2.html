State predictors on V2

Our state predictors, trained only on the simple set, run on Dataset V2's furnished rooms they have never seen. A first qualitative look; updated 09 November 2026, 07:31 UTC.

In short

  1. Furnished rooms cost about 2.5 times the error. regression 100M, chain of 10: 0.16 m on the simple set's test rooms, 0.40 m on V2, per clip.
  2. Invented motion rises too: 19.0% of still objects moved on the simple set, 31.5% on V2.
  3. Hardest on V2: orbital (1.94 m), rocket (1.37 m), coupled springs (1.22 m), balloon (1.03 m). Easiest: books ball catch (0.04 m), trampoline (0.04 m), crate drop table (0.05 m), diving board (0.07 m).
  4. The models never saw furniture or these rooms in training; this is how far they carry over, before any training on V2.

Action evaluation · given a simulator, does the planner find an action that works in PhysX?
  1. What changed since the first scene-graph run: contacts are read from the poses for PhysX and predicted motion alike (checked against PhysX's own contact reports, block below), each scene gets a second task about contacts where one exists (37 here), the prompt says which names are action dimensions and shows the range the rig really applies, an answer that cannot be run gets the allowed answers back, and the four anti-shake predictors are extra simulators. Same 90 main tasks, same planner.
  2. With PhysX in the loop it meets the main outcome on 60 and 65 of 90 tasks (two runs). First scene-graph run: 65 and 64; JSON scene: 63 and 59. With no simulator: 8.
  3. Met in PhysX with a predictor (in brackets, also said met by the predictor): non-block flow 28 (21), non-block regression 26 (18), masked regression (all questions) 25 (13), masked flow (all questions) 23 (17). Anti-shake versions: non-block regression 39 (21), masked regression 34 (11), non-block flow 33 (23), masked flow 22 (16).
  4. Non-block regression fell from 39 to 26 against the first scene-graph run (4 gained, 17 lost, p = 0.007), and the planner caused it: the predictor's outcomes on the same actions are identical in both runs, and so is the feedback. This time the planner searched worse; on drop 1 it kept the release height between 0.10 and 0.23 m although the goal needs about 0.26. The two PhysX runs alone differ on 21 to 29 tasks against the first run.
  5. Anti-shake predictors, a hint: non-block regression anti-shake against its base gains 17 and loses 4 (p = 0.007). Over all four predictors, anti-shake gains 53 tasks and loses 27 on V2 (24 and 18 on the simple set). Neither survives the 28 comparisons, so it needs a repeat.
  6. Contact tasks: PhysX meets 27 and 26 of 37, the predictors 2 to 7. Each predictor says "met" on 19 to 30 of them, and PhysX confirms 2 to 5.
  7. Without the LLM, a predictor's met / missed verdict agrees with PhysX on 71-79% of main-task values and 48-65% of contact-task values. Regression ranks a task's outcomes as PhysX does (median rank correlation 0.81-0.83); flow does not (0.24-0.48).
  8. Answers that could not be run, PhysX arms: 74 of 1,722 attempts (first scene-graph run 106 of 1,667, JSON 26 of 1,659). Most are values just outside the shown range.

How this was done · the tasks, the scene graph, the loop, contacts, the verifier and the bridge
  • Task: the scene of a clip on this tab and an outcome its own action misses, for example "objects toppled exactly 6". The outcome comes from simulating eight values of the scene's main action in PhysX, so the working values are known. Each scene also gets a contact task where one exists: whether the acted-on body touches a given object, or how high it rebounds after its first impact.
  • What the planner reads: a scene graph of the first frame only. One record per object (alike objects share a record): its colour, shape, mass, friction, bounce, centre, lowest and highest point. Then relations: what rests on what, what is inside the container, what touches, the joints, and where the other objects lie from the acted-on one. Then the answer format and the action options in physical units (for example the push force in N it multiplies). The ranges are the values the rig applies on that scene (found by compiling, rounded inward), and the prompt says which names are dimensions.
  • Loop: the planner (Qwen with the SFT and DPO stages, trained on the JSON scene only) proposes an action. The simulator's motion is turned into an outcome graph: per object, how far it moved, where it ended and on what, inside the container or not, the highest point, toppled or not, and the contacts of the goal's objects after the start. The planner gets that graph and the check of the required outcome; an answer that could not be run gets the allowed answers back. It tries up to 20 times and stops when the check says met.
  • Contacts: read from the poses for PhysX and predicted motion alike. Each object's collision parts (as the predictors encode them) give a gap per frame (exact convex distance); a pair touches when the gap is 3 mm or less and separates beyond 8 mm, and a bounce on the floor that falls between two frames is found from the closing speed. Calibrated against PhysX's own contact reports (block below).
  • Oscillations count a swing only when the body leaves a band of max(1 cm, 5% of its swing) around its mean position on both sides: the predictors' 12-frame block seams crossed the mean and doubled their counts.
  • Verifier: the required outcome is a condition on the outcome graph, for example "block: toppled = no" or "actor: touched block after the start = yes". The same code builds the graph from PhysX motion and from predicted motion. On all 4,953 PhysX rollouts of the tasks' scenes it gives the same verdict and value as the measurement rule (4,953 of 4,953).
  • Bridge to the state predictors: a graph object is the scene object of the same id, and its predictor slot is that id's place in the predictor's input. An action is compiled by code into the scene and then into the predictor's input tokens; no second model is involved. All 1,388 movable objects of the 189 tasks have a slot, and every slot an object.
  • Truth: every final action is replayed in PhysX, and the verifier on its outcome graph decides success. So a predictor can say "met" and be wrong.
