End-to-end study

From LLM-written physics tasks to choosing actions with a learned simulator, with PhysX as the ground truth at every step.

Two LLMs write physics tasks for scenes they design in Isaac Sim. PhysX checks every task and is the ground truth for everything below: which tasks are valid, how hard they are, whether a reviewer's verdict is right, and whether a chosen action succeeds.

Status: wave 1 rebuilt: 165 valid test tasks; all sections filled with four LLM proposers and three trained future-state models; updated 13 Oct 2026, 10:53. Sections fill in as runs finish.

Key results

  • GPT-5.4 is the strongest zero-shot action proposer: its first choice succeeds on 56% of tasks, against 35% for Qwen3-VL-30B Thinking and 12% for Qwen3-VL-30B Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B, which do worse than one random action (19%).
  • GPT-5.4 is also the best scene writer (10 of 10 scenes built, against 0-2 of 10 for Qwen3-VL-30B) and a reliable reviewer when it sees object states and the simulator's definitions (89% agreement with PhysX on its own tasks).
  • The trained future-state models learn the moved object's motion but not what happens to the objects it hits. As action choosers they lift 64 random candidates from 19% to 33-35% and GPT-5.4's candidates from 56% to 59%, far below what PhysX itself reaches (93% and 68%).

4606 test rollouts on the task-suite scenes (the action pickers' candidates), none seen in training; errors over the first 3 s, median over rollouts. "Objects it hits": objects other than the moved one that really move more than 2 cm.

  • On the moved object, the Transformer (0.19 m) and Diffusion (0.22 m) beat "nothing moves" (0.28 m) over the first 3 s; the MLP does not (0.32 m).
  • None predicts the objects that get hit (0.30-0.36 m, no better than predicting no motion), and they drift still objects by 2-8 cm; so they recognize only 6-23% of real successes (Diffusion the most).
  • Likely causes: the training scenes are variants of five seed scenes while the test scenes are GPT-5.4's designs, and collisions are rare in random training actions. The 353 chain-reaction training rollouts are not in these models yet.

Setup

  • Actions (one per task, applied at t = 0): place, drop, throw (position + velocity), push (velocity), spin (angular velocity). PhysX runs 3 s at 120 Hz.
  • Task writers: GPT-5.4 (Azure, default reasoning) and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B; each designs scenes, then one task per category per scene. Success conditions are trees of checkable predicates (distance, contact, inside, on top of, toppled, at rest, ...).
  • Future-state models: same inputs (object tokens: shape, size, mass, friction, bounciness, start pose, action) and output (each movable object's position, orientation and contacts every 0.1 s for 11 s). Trained on rollouts from scenes that are not in the task suite.
  • Numbers on this page are refreshed as runs finish; sections without enough data say so.
  • Each task's action space follows its instruction: throw, push and spin start where the object rests unless the instruction says otherwise; place and drop release points are chosen anywhere or in a stated region; the heading is chosen only in orient tasks. Planners see only the scene and the instruction.
  • Difficulty: share of 64 random actions from the task's action space that succeed. Easy: at least 13 of 64; hard: at most 5 of 64, including tasks solved only by 160 refinement tries; medium in between. Tasks with no solution found are listed separately.
