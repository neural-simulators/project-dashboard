Improved Results V2

Iteration 2: step-wise prediction against objects passing through each other: the learned simulators of the Scaled results tab with physical structure and rules added, on the same test set and the same scenes.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

What changed (15 Oct 2026, trained and evaluated)↑ contents

In every model so far, original or Improved V1, a pushed traffic cone ends up 8-24 cm inside a wall instead of bouncing off a pole, and a dropped basketball sinks into a bucket. The geometry is in the inputs (each object's 3D box and pieces), but the models predict the whole 3 s at once and never compare one object's box with another's. Here the 3 s are predicted 0.2 s at a time, each block starting from the state the previous block ended in, as learned particle and mesh simulators do (GNS, MeshGraphNets, FIGNet); optionally with the relative geometry of the 8 nearest objects' 3D boxes recomputed at every block. All runs start from the MOVi + Physion pretraining and keep hold still.

VariantWhat it adds
One-shot, pretrainedthe whole 3 s in one pass (Scaled results; the starting point of these runs)
One-shot + hold stillImproved V1's best change
Step-wise0.2 s blocks chained from the predicted state; hold still per block
+ box geometryfor each moving object and its 8 nearest objects: gap between their 3D boxes, contact direction, relative position, rotation and velocity, approach speed and time to impact, recomputed at every block (attention bias and messages)
+ noisesmall noise on moving objects' start state during training (5 mm, 5 cm/s), so errors do not build up over the 15 blocks

The one-shot rows are the baselines; each step-wise version adds its change on top of the one above. Step-wise runs: 20,000 training steps from the MOVi + Physion pretrained weights, 8 random blocks per training simulation.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • Step-wise prediction gives the most accurate learned simulators so far. Regression with box geometry averages 0.434 m on moving objects, against 0.493 m one-shot pretrained and 0.608 m for the original (12% and 29% lower); flow matching 0.546 m, against 0.627 m and 0.748 m (13% and 27% lower). Task verdicts agree with the physics engine 90.7% of the time against 88.2%.
  • The motion is physically cleaner: back-and-forth without contact 2.9 -> 0.01 per moving object, energy from nowhere 2.6 -> 0.8 J, bystanders moved 63% -> 3%, drift of objects that stay still 6.5 -> 1.0 cm.
  • Passing through is reduced but not solved: regression 7.4 -> 4.5 cm deeper than reality on average, flow matching 6.4 -> 4.3 cm; simulations with a pair more than 5 cm deeper fall from about 50% to 31-32%, much of it already from hold still (V1: 36%, flow 48%). In the clips every model still pushes the traffic cone into or through the wall, and the basketball does not knock the bucket over.
  • The box geometry of the 8 nearest objects improves accuracy a little (regression 0.453 -> 0.434 m, flow matching 0.595 -> 0.546 m) but not passing through (4.6 -> 4.5 cm). Noise on the start state does not help (0.448 m).
  • Not fixed: free-flight error stays about 9.3 m/s2 in every model (objects in the air do not fall).
  • What follows: iteration 3 adds a no-penetration loss, the contact law, a corner loss and collision guidance while sampling (World model items WM-F3 to WM-F6); point clouds only if these fail (WM-F7).

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
difference between the predicted acceleration of an object in free flight and gravity (m/s²; 0 in reality).
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
