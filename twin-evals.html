v3: the first evaluation

Launches turned toward the room, every chair and table movable, the movable twins before the rig; clips in the physics stage's colours. Kept for comparison.

In short

  1. Barely better than standing still. Over the bodies PhysX moves, the models are off by 0.62 to 0.68 m on average, against 0.82 m for a prediction where nothing moves. On the same four families in their own test scenes they are off by 0.17 to 0.24 m.
  2. They move furniture that stays still. In 51 to 66 of 70 records a model moves twin furniture PhysX keeps still; the regression model most (still bodies drift up to 0.56 m), the flow model least (0.20 m).
  3. The masked flow model is best on the twins (ADE 0.62 m, the same topples as PhysX in 83% of records), although the regression model is best in the families' own test scenes.
  4. Crate drops go well, tips do not. Crate drops: 0.17 m against 0.28 m standing still. Chair and table tips are no better than standing still.

Each record is one action in one twin: a training family's rig (chair spill, table topple, projectile, crate drop) placed among the scanned furniture, with the family's own action (a0) and the same action at the top of its range (a1). The model reads the first frame and the program and predicts 5 s; PhysX runs the same scene with the furniture's real colliders. 70 records, 35 scenarios.

The clips: 17 of the 70 records, picked greedily so every room, every scenario, both action strengths, toppling and not toppling, and good and bad predictions appear (at most three per scenario, every room at least twice). Each grid: PhysX, then the four models on the same camera and clock; each label gives that model's error (ADE over the bodies PhysX moves), and the last panel the record's numbers. Rendered in the paper look (path tracing); the scanned twins keep the colours of their physics stage, static scanned furniture is drawn as its fitted box, the outdoor scenes get a sky and a sun. A model's body that sinks below the floor is lifted back onto it in the clips (deeper than PhysX's own box ever goes); the numbers are for the raw predictions. Clips play on hover or tap.
