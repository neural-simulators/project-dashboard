Physics-guided predictors

Learned simulators break basic physics: objects pass through each other, bystanders drift, pendulum arms stretch. Here we add physical structure and a few universal rules, one iteration at a time, and measure what each change buys.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every iteration is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

How to read the tables

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
difference between the predicted acceleration of an object in free flight and gravity (m/s²; 0 in reality).
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).

Iteration 1: hold still what does not move, integrate motion, four universal rules (14 Oct 2026, running)

In the comparison clips of the Scaled results tab, every learned simulator breaks basic physics: dominoes next to a swinging pendulum shuffle with nothing touching them, a pendulum arm changes length, a cone slides through a wall, and objects reverse direction in mid-air. Measured over the whole test set, predicted objects overlap by 0.15-0.24 m at the worst moment (the physics engine: about 0.005 m), the acceleration in free flight is off from gravity by about 10 m/s2, and 2-4 J of energy appear from nowhere per simulation. Because every architecture and training objective shows this, the fix has to be structural.

What changed

VariantWhat it adds
Originalthe model as trained in the Scaled results tab (retrained on the same, larger data)
+ Hold stillthe model also predicts, for each object, whether it will move at all; objects predicted not to move are kept exactly where they are after the action
+ Integrate motionthe model predicts accelerations (zero by default, so an untouched object stays put); velocities and positions are added up from the state right after the action, so positions always match velocities and nothing reverses without a cause
+ Rulesfour penalties during training: nothing below the floor; no more overlap between objects than in the real motion; rigid joints (pendulum arms, rope links) keep their length; an object that is not acted on stays put until it really starts moving
+ Clearer inputseach object's row also carries its pose and velocity right after the action, and each predicted time chunk gets its actual time
