Updated 27 Oct 2026, 11:57.

Last week in one minute↑ contents

  • Tried: multi-block (chain) training at 3, 5, 10 and 15 blocks for regression and flow matching; model size 23M, 50M, 100M; exact convex geometry (the real collision shapes as face planes and hull points) against boxes; generation rules; longer training; 19 multi-step flow variants; flow on top of regression; engine continuations for flow; a 6-s test with event tags. GRPO and DPO were discussed and dropped, not run.
  • Worked: chains for regression, and longer is better: test error 0.403 m (3 blocks) to 0.372 m (15 blocks), the same as the best model so far (0.371 m, 50M with exact geometry and rules) at half the size. Exact geometry, 50M and the generation rules each help too.
  • Did not work: chains for flow matching (0.494 m without chains, 0.816 m with 15 blocks); 100M (no better than 50M at the same steps); flow on top of regression; engine continuations.
  • Why regression and not flow: given the full scene (every pose, velocity, mass, friction, shape and the action) the next 0.2 s has one answer, so the spread flow adds is pure error, and it compounds over a chain. Flow helps camera-pose estimation because a few images do not decide the pose (symmetric objects, hidden sides) and its metric (within 15 degrees) rewards picking one answer; our metric is distance, which rewards the single best guess.
  • 6-s test: error more than doubles after 3 s; blocks with a hit, landing or topple add 2.5 times the error of a quiet block; the 15-block regression model is best over all 6 s.

Exact convex geometry against boxes↑ contents

Exact geometry: each object's real collision shape (convex face planes, hull points, mesh pieces) as the model's input instead of a box. It reads contacts right (false overlaps 12.5% to 2.5%), helps when trained from scratch (0.393 to 0.382 m with chains and rules) but not when fine-tuned from a box-trained model.
