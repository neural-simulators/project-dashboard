Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

What changed (16 Oct 2026, trained and evaluated, clips rendered; block diffusion and reviewer study added)↑ contents

Learned simulators break pendulums and springs: the arm changes length, the bob flies off, a hanging weight drops off its spring. V5 adds the equations of motion for objects on a revolute joint fixed to the world (a pendulum, with its centre of mass and inertia) and on a prismatic joint with a drive (a spring plunger, with its limits), integrated in 16 sub-steps per frame, as the built-in motion the network corrects (like built-in gravity). Trained on every training scene (357 scenes, 134,265 simulations) from V4's best overfitting-stage model, 20,000 steps, and compared with the original V3 (all scenes) and the new V3 (trained without pendulum, spring and soft-body scenes) on the same fixed random 15,000 of all test simulations.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • V5 has the lowest position error so far on all test scenes: regression 0.435 m against 0.459 m for the original V3 (5% lower), flow matching 0.555 m against 0.569 m (2% lower); verdicts agree with the physics engine about 90% of the time, as before.
  • The gain is largest where the equations apply (regression): pendulum scenes 0.397 -> 0.349 m (12% lower) with the arm stretching 0.4 cm instead of 1.0 cm; spring scenes 0.512 -> 0.494 m with joint stretch 3.4 -> 1.6 cm; soft-body scenes 0.238 -> 0.231 m; other scenes 0.490 -> 0.471 m.
  • Flow matching gains on pendulums (0.459 -> 0.426 m) and springs (0.642 -> 0.592 m) but is worse on soft bodies (0.286 -> 0.319 m) and stretches springs more (5.6 -> 9.5 cm).
  • As in V3 and V4: toppling recall rises (regression 25% -> 52%), tilt error is a little higher (26.6 -> 28.2 degrees), and passing through rises (3.0 -> 4.0 cm; with guidance 3.1 cm).
  • Clips: V5 (regression) keeps the pendulum bob on its arm and the hanging weight on its spring, close to the physics engine; the original V3 separates the bob from the arm and lets the weight drop; the new V3, which never saw them, throws both off. V5 flow matching keeps the bob hanging but knocks over the nearby domino stack, and pulls the weight away from its spring. No model keeps the jelly cube together when the tray hits it.
  • Correction: the free-flight error column (about 9 m/s² for every model) is a measurement fault; the corrected free-fall error is in the block diffusion section below (V5 regression 1.9 m/s², flow matching 2.0 m/s²).

Block diffusion against flow matching and regression (16 Oct 2026)↑ contents

V5's flow-matching model predicts each 0.2 s block by flow matching (the network outputs the clean block; 10 sampling steps per block). Here the same model is trained as classic denoising diffusion instead: a cosine noise schedule and DDIM sampling with 10 steps per block, on the change from the built-in motion, with the same data (all training scenes), settings, starting weights and 20,000 steps. Three versions of what the network predicts were first checked for 3,000 steps on the overfitting stage (four scenes): the noise (classic DDPM), the clean block, and v (Salimans & Ho 2022).

What changed

VariantWhat it adds
V5 regressionthe network predicts each block directly (no noise)
V5 flow matchingflow matching per block (Improved Results V5)
diffusion, noise predictionclassic DDPM (Ho et al. 2020): the network predicts the added noise; overfitting check only (it fails, below)
diffusion, clean blockthe network predicts the clean block (as in motion diffusion models, e.g. MDM)
diffusion, vthe network predicts v, a mix of noise and clean block (Salimans & Ho 2022)

Overfitting-stage check, validation error at 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 steps: noise prediction 1.57 / 1.83 / 2.03 m (objects that should stay still drift 2.6 m; with still objects weighted fully 1.56 / 1.82 / 2.09 m); clean block 1.12 / 0.82 / 0.64 m; v 1.38 / 0.98 / 0.72 m; flow matching (on an 8,000-step schedule) 1.13 / 0.71 / 0.41 m.

Takeaways

  • Flow matching is clearly better than classic diffusion here: position error 0.555 m against 0.748 m (diffusion, clean block) and 0.804 m (diffusion, v); regression stays best (0.435 m). The validation curves level off at 0.72 and 0.75 m against 0.49 m for flow matching, so longer training would not close the gap.
  • Classic noise prediction does not work for chained blocks: its training loss falls normally, but the noise cannot be predicted at low noise levels, so each block ends with leftover noise (also in velocities); the next block starts from that state and the error grows over the 15 blocks (2.03 m after 3,000 steps of the overfitting check, still objects drifting 2.6 m). Starting the sampler below the highest noise level or weighting still objects fully does not help; predicting the clean block or v does.
  • Diffusion (clean block) moves fewer bystanders than flow matching (3.2% against 5.3%) and stretches joints less (1.4 cm against 2.2 cm); passing through is similar (4.2 against 4.6 cm), verdicts agree a little less often (88.9% against 89.5%) and fewer topples are caught (45% against 56%).
  • Objects in free fall: regression and flow matching stay about 2 m/s² from gravity (1.9 and 2.0 m/s²; the physics engine scores 0.02), so built-in gravity works for them; diffusion is far off (6.5 m/s² clean block, 7.4 m/s² v).
  • Clips: diffusion (clean block) keeps the pendulum bob on its arm, like regression, where flow matching knocks the domino stack over and diffusion (v) throws the bob up; in the chain scene only diffusion (clean block) ends the die and the ball near their true places; every model pushes the traffic cone into the wall.

Reviewers judging predicted motion (16 Oct 2026)↑ contents

Can a learned simulator and an AI reviewer replace the physics engine and the rule checks when deciding whether a task succeeded? The same 1,520 reviewer items as Scaled results 1.5 (418 test tasks; per task two real successes and two failures, one of them a near miss). Each reviewer gets the same evidence as there (object states at 0, 1, 2 and 3 s, first and last touches, the task and the action; no images), computed from a V5 model's predicted motion instead of the physics engine's. Two references: the physics engine with the rule checks (the truth), and the rule checks applied to the same predicted motion (does the reviewer read that motion correctly?).

Each reviewer is scored on the items it answered for every kind of motion (GPT-5.4 1,520; Gemma-4-31B 1,512; Qwen3.8-27B 1,494; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 1,431). Real successes found: share of the physics engine's successes the judge calls a success; real failures found: the same for failures.

Takeaways

  • The learned simulator is the bottleneck, not the reviewer: on predicted motion every reviewer agrees with the physics engine only 61-67% of the time (κ 0.19-0.30), about as often as the rule checks on that motion (66-68%); the same reviewers agree 80-91% when they see the physics engine's motion.
  • Reviewers read predicted motion as well as real motion: GPT-5.4 agrees with the rule checks on the same predicted motion 94-96% of the time (κ 0.83-0.87), against 91% on the physics engine's motion; Gemma-4-31B 91-92%, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 89%, Qwen3.8-27B 85%.
  • Predicted motion misses real successes: only 29-43% of them are found (rule checks: 37-42%), while 79-92% of failures are recognised. A judge built on a learned simulator would under-reward rather than over-reward.
  • Every reviewer ranks the simulators the same way: regression and flow matching about equal, diffusion 1-3 points lower.
  • Open models: Gemma-4-31B comes closest to GPT-5.4 (65-66% against 66-67% with PhysX on predicted motion; 86% against 91% on the physics engine's motion); Qwen3.8-27B calls the most failures successes (79-83% of failures found); Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is the fastest.

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): it counted objects resting on the floor, or on anything whose contact the model missed, as in free flight, so every model and the physics engine itself score about 8-10. Ignore this column; the free-fall error replaces it.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
Free-fall error
how far the predicted vertical acceleration, measured over 0.1 s, is from gravity on stretches where the physics engine's own motion is a free fall (its vertical acceleration within 0.5 m/s² of gravity for 7 frames in a row); m/s². The physics engine itself scores 0.02, and 2 mm of position jitter adds about 0.4.
