Improved Results V5

Iteration 5: V3's model with the equations of a pendulum and a spring built in, trained on every scene type, on the same test simulations.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

What changed (16 Oct 2026, trained and evaluated, clips rendered)↑ contents

Learned simulators break pendulums and springs: the arm changes length, the bob flies off, a hanging weight drops off its spring. V5 adds the equations of motion for objects on a revolute joint fixed to the world (a pendulum, with its centre of mass and inertia) and on a prismatic joint with a drive (a spring plunger, with its limits), integrated in 16 sub-steps per frame, as the built-in motion the network corrects (like built-in gravity). Trained on every training scene (357 scenes, 134,265 simulations) from V4's best overfitting-stage model, 20,000 steps, and compared with the original V3 (all scenes) and the new V3 (trained without pendulum, spring and soft-body scenes) on the same fixed random 15,000 of all test simulations.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • V5 has the lowest position error so far on all test scenes: regression 0.435 m against 0.459 m for the original V3 (5% lower), flow matching 0.555 m against 0.569 m (2% lower); verdicts agree with the physics engine about 90% of the time, as before.
  • The gain is largest where the equations apply (regression): pendulum scenes 0.397 -> 0.349 m (12% lower) with the arm stretching 0.4 cm instead of 1.0 cm; spring scenes 0.512 -> 0.494 m with joint stretch 3.4 -> 1.6 cm; soft-body scenes 0.238 -> 0.231 m; other scenes 0.490 -> 0.471 m.
  • Flow matching gains on pendulums (0.459 -> 0.426 m) and springs (0.642 -> 0.592 m) but is worse on soft bodies (0.286 -> 0.319 m) and stretches springs more (5.6 -> 9.5 cm).
  • As in V3 and V4: toppling recall rises (regression 25% -> 52%), tilt error is a little higher (26.6 -> 28.2 degrees), and passing through rises (3.0 -> 4.0 cm; with guidance 3.1 cm).
  • Clips: V5 (regression) keeps the pendulum bob on its arm and the hanging weight on its spring, close to the physics engine; the original V3 separates the bob from the arm and lets the weight drop; the new V3, which never saw them, throws both off. V5 flow matching keeps the bob hanging but knocks over the nearby domino stack, and pulls the weight away from its spring. No model keeps the jelly cube together when the tray hits it.
  • Correction: the free-flight error column (about 9 m/s² for every model) is a measurement fault, not a failure to fall; see How to read the tables.

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
difference between the predicted acceleration of an object in free flight and gravity (m/s²; 0 in reality). Faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): floor contacts are not recorded, so objects resting on the floor counted as falling and every model, like the physics engine itself, scores about 9-10. Fixed for later evaluations; ignore this column until then.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
