Checkpoint transfer to Dataset V2

Can the old neural simulators predict new physical scenes?

Five frozen regression and flow checkpoints predict motion from the original initial scene and action of each unseen V2 example. Their outputs are compared with the saved PhysX trajectories. One case per supported family makes this an exploratory transfer comparison.

11 of 52 families scored · 55 of 55 primary predictions completed · 41 families unsupported by the action interface. Five additional moving-pulley predictions are reported separately.

Condition: 30 Hz, original start, all four generation rules. Background triangle meshes are represented by static boxes; foreground collision descriptions are retained. Context is capped at 64 objects. The original reference physics is unchanged. Full rollouts last 5-8 seconds; the first three seconds are also scored. Longer rollouts chain 0.2-second blocks with the trained time input capped.

Case macro means. Moving-object and orientation columns use 10 cases; all-body columns use 11. The selected pulley reference has no body translating more than 1 cm, so it contributes to all-body error but has no moving-object error. Position ADE averages framewise distance; FDE measures endpoint distance, in metres. The all-body ADE also penalizes motion hallucinated on bodies that stay still. Orientation is mean angular error in degrees for non-spheres. Lower is better. These are not task success rates.

Coverage of all 52 motion families

A family remains unsupported when none of its audited valid factual variants has an encodable action program. The audit covers 429 completed roots and 1,282 factual records, including 1,267 physics-valid records. One valid factual representative was selected from each of the 11 action-compatible families.

What was measured, and what was approximated?

Selection was frozen before predictions. It prefers an available measured rest time within four seconds, then stable record-id order. Outcome misses remain eligible. All checkpoints use their shared EMA weights and ten sampling steps per block. Every prediction starts at the recorded initial state; future states are used only for scoring.

Untyped time-zero velocity boundaries use the legacy throw token when linear velocity is nonzero, spin when only angular velocity is nonzero, and drop otherwise. Their numerical values are unchanged. Records without an action keep the no-action input. Timed actuator programs are unsupported.

The rule setting enables floor projection, contact projection, a motion-cause gate and analytic priors. These modify generation; the output is explicitly a model-plus-rules condition. Even the co-author's plain condition includes floor projection, and these checkpoints have built-in gravity and joint priors.

Static background furniture uses its local bounding box. The geometry checkpoints retain their bounded convex/primitive representation for foreground objects. The contact-material fallback was independently verified in the pinned engine: friction 0.5, restitution 0, with exact fixture agreement against explicit material values. Recorded dynamic masses, inertia and centre of mass are preserved. Per-case context omissions appear in the explorer.

A reference-moving body moves more than 0.01 m from its scene pose within the measurement window. The given initial frame is excluded from errors. Stationary spinning bodies can have small position error despite poor orientation. Additional per-case measures include velocity RMSE and contact-pair-frame F1; reference contacts are the union of the four 120 Hz intervals inside each 30 Hz interval.
