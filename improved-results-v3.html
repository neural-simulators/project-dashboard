Improved Results V3

Iteration 3: contact terms and collision guidance: the learned simulators of the Scaled results tab with physical structure and rules added, on the same test set and the same scenes.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

What changed (15 Oct 2026, trained and evaluated; retrained 16 Oct 2026 (section below))↑ contents

V2's step-wise models are the most accurate so far, but a pushed cone still goes through a wall, a dropped basketball does not knock a bucket over, and toppling is caught only about half the time (rotation was in the loss only weakly and never measured). Here, built on V2's best (step-wise + box geometry, from the MOVi + Physion pretraining, hold still): collision guidance while sampling, and three training terms.

VariantWhat it adds
V2 beststep-wise + box geometry (Improved Results V2)
V2 + guidancewhile sampling, every denoising step is nudged down the gradient of the overlap between objects' 3D boxes and their neighbours' pieces, measured along the contact direction from before the overlap (so an object half way through a wall is pushed back); regression gets 5 such correction steps after its prediction. No retraining (MotionDiffuser, CTG)
+ no-penetration losstraining penalises overlap deeper than in the real motion (beyond 1 cm), every frame, boxes against the neighbours' largest pieces
+ contact lawan object inside another by more than 5 mm must not keep moving inward along the contact normal (the condition physics engines enforce at every contact), plus double weight on objects that make a new contact during a block
+ corner lossdistance between each object's 8 predicted and true box corners: position and orientation in metres, so a toppled tall object counts fully (point-matching loss of 6D pose estimation)

V2 best and V2 + guidance are the same trained model; each loss version adds its term on top of the one above, trained like V2 (20,000 steps from the pretrained weights). New measures: rotation error (balls excluded), tilt error (ignores spin about the vertical), and toppling: of the objects upright after the action, those ending tilted more than 45 degrees.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • The no-penetration loss is the clearest gain against passing through so far: regression 4.5 -> 2.9 cm deeper than reality on average (pairs more than 5 cm deeper: 31% -> 21% of simulations), flow matching 4.3 -> 3.0 cm (32% -> 23%), for 4% more position error (0.434 -> 0.452 m; 0.546 -> 0.567 m).
  • The contact law (no moving inward while inside another object) adds nothing measurable on top (2.9 cm), but in the cone clip it makes the cone stop upright in front of the wall instead of grazing into it.
  • Rotation is not improved by any term: tilt error stays about 27-30 degrees, as for the one-shot model (26.5). The no-penetration loss alone lowers toppling recall (regression 43% -> 25%, flow matching 38% -> 28%); the contact law and the corner loss bring it back to 36-44% but do not lower the tilt error. A rotation ablation (V4) is running, starting with an overfitting stage on four scenes.
  • Collision guidance (no retraining) beats the no-penetration loss against passing through: on the same 15,000 test simulations, regression pairs more than 5 cm deeper than reality fall from 32% to 10% of simulations (loss: 21%) for 2% more position error; flow matching from 33% to 16% (loss: 22%) with slightly lower position error (0.549 -> 0.535 m). Neither helps rotation, and guidance lowers toppling recall a little (43% -> 36% regression).

Retrained V3: V4's best and built-in gravity, without pendulum, spring and soft-body scenes (16 Oct 2026)↑ contents

The original V3 above was trained on every training scene. The new V3 starts from V4's best overfitting-stage model (corners as outputs, built-in gravity at the centre of mass, no-penetration loss, box geometry of the 8 nearest objects, hold still) and is trained only on the training scenes without a pendulum, spring or soft body (304 of 357 scenes, 108,514 simulations), 20,000 steps. Both are compared on the same fixed random 15,000 test simulations from scenes without those mechanisms, and on 15,000 of all test simulations.

original V3the no-penetration model above (all training scenes)
original V2 + guidanceV2's step-wise model with collision guidance while sampling (the best of the original V3 tab against passing through)
new V3V4's best + built-in gravity, retrained without pendulum, spring and soft-body scenes
new V3 + guidancethe same with collision guidance while sampling (8 nudges per denoising step; regression: 5 correction steps)

Takeaways

  • Position error is unchanged: regression 0.475 m against 0.478 m for the original V3, flow matching 0.617 m against 0.608 m; task verdicts agree with the physics engine 90% of the time for both.
  • Toppling is caught far more often, as in V4: recall 31% -> 54% (regression) and 34% -> 57% (flow matching), with lower precision (68% -> 56%, 54% -> 46%).
  • Rotation and passing through are worse: tilt error 29.3 -> 31.8 degrees, passing through 3.1 -> 4.6 cm (simulations above 5 cm 23% -> 33%); guidance takes it back to 3.7 cm, still above the original V2 + guidance (2.1 cm). In V4 corners as outputs also added about 0.5 cm of passing through.
  • On all test scenes the new V3 is worse (0.490 m against 0.459 m; bystanders moved 11% against 3%), as expected for a model that never saw pendulums, springs or soft bodies: those are V5's job.
  • Clips: the new V3 (regression) stops the traffic cone at the wall, where the original pushes it into the wall; flow matching still pushes it in. The new V3 scatters the rope into pieces (a soft body it never saw).
  • Correction: the free-flight error column (about 9 m/s² for every model) is a measurement fault; the corrected free-fall error is in the block diffusion section of Improved Results V5 (V5 regression 1.9 m/s², flow matching 2.0 m/s²).

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): it counted objects resting on the floor, or on anything whose contact the model missed, as in free flight, so every model and the physics engine itself score about 8-10. Ignore this column; the free-fall error replaces it.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
Free-fall error
how far the predicted vertical acceleration, measured over 0.1 s, is from gravity on stretches where the physics engine's own motion is a free fall (its vertical acceleration within 0.5 m/s² of gravity for 7 frames in a row); m/s². The physics engine itself scores 0.02, and 2 mm of position jitter adds about 0.4.
