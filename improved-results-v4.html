Iteration 4: which change helps rotation (toppling), position error and passing through most, tested first by over-fitting four training scenes.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

What changed (15 Oct 2026, stage 1 and stage 2 evaluated, clips rendered)↑ contents

Every model so far has a tilt error of about 27 degrees on the test set and catches only about half of the real topples. Before training on all the data, each rotation change is tested where the model should be able to fit: four training scenes (a brick slid toward a wall, with 1,132 wall hits; a bucket, pin, crate and golf ball; a skittle knocked over; a domino chain), about 19,300 simulations made for this (many actions per scene, each action also with one parameter varied: speed, direction, height, contact point). 80% of the simulations of each scene train the model; 20% are held out (actions not seen in that scene). Base for every variant: step-wise + box geometry + no-penetration loss, with hold still counting a turn of more than 2 degrees as moving.

VariantWhat it adds
baseV3's no-penetration model; hold still now counts turning as moving (a topple starts with little centre motion)
+ rotation as a changerotation predicted as the change from the block start, scaled like positions
+ angle lossthe angle between predicted and true orientation as its own loss (weight 20)
+ orientation from spinorientation integrated from the predicted spin over the block
+ symmetric axis + topple weightfor objects symmetric about their axis only the axis is supervised, balls not at all; triple weight on blocks where an object tips by more than 10 degrees; with the angle loss
corners as outputsthe network outputs each object's 8 box corners; position and orientation follow by a rigid fit (keypoints instead of rotation numbers, as in pose estimation)
change + angle + symmetric + topplesall but spin and corners together
base on 50% / 25% of the datalearning curve: does more data of the same scenes help?

Every variant: 8,000 training steps from the MOVi + Physion pretrained weights, regression and flow matching.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • Corners as outputs gives the largest gain: the only change that lowers position error for both objectives (regression 0.263 -> 0.241 m, flow matching 0.338 -> 0.327 m on held-out actions), and it roughly doubles toppling recall (regression 41% -> 67%, flow matching 32% -> 74%), with lower precision (82% -> 75%, 80% -> 65%); passing through rises by about 0.5 cm, which guidance can take back.
  • In the clips the gain of corners as outputs is visible where toppling matters: when a thrown bowling pin knocks the bucket over, only corners as outputs tips the bucket onto its side as the physics engine does; every other variant leaves it standing. On the barrel spun into the wall no variant is right; the variants with the angle loss scatter or freeze pieces in the skittle and domino scenes.
  • Rotation as a change from the block start also raises toppling recall (to 62-63%) and slightly lowers tilt error, but costs position error (+11% regression, +7% flow matching). Orientation from spin helps regression's position (0.249 m) but has the worst tilt error.
  • The angle loss gives the lowest tilt error (16.9 degrees, regression) but at weight 20 it drowns the position terms (0.389 m); with the symmetric-axis loss and in the combination the same happens: the weight needs to be much smaller.
  • No variant fits even its training actions: errors on training and held-out actions are the same (about 0.24-0.34 m), and 25% of the data is almost as good as all of it. The loss on single 0.2 s blocks is small; the error comes from chaining 15 blocks, each starting from the model's own previous prediction, and grows fastest around collisions. The next step is to train on several chained blocks at once (the model's own predictions as starts, as in GNS's pushforward and self-forcing).

Stage 2: built-in physics and Newton's laws, same four scenes (16 Oct 2026)↑ contents

Starting from stage 1's best (corners as outputs), each physics rule is added on the same four scenes, to see which one improves the measure it targets without costing position error. Also tested: more training data of fast spins (the barrel spun into the wall was the worst clip of stage 1). Two test sets: the held-out actions of stage 1 (3,835 simulations) and held-out fast spins (1,118 simulations of objects spun at 10-50 rad/s, not used for training).

What changed

VariantWhat it adds
V4 best (reference)stage 1's corners as outputs
+ built-in gravityan object touching nothing falls by the equations of free flight from its centre of mass and turns freely with constant angular momentum; the network predicts only the correction
+ velocity from motionvelocity and spin taken from the change of the predicted poses instead of a separate output
+ relative overlapno-penetration loss divided by the thinnest side of the moving object (weight 2), so 2 cm into a tray counts more than 2 cm into a table
+ moving in truth or predictionthe position loss also counts objects the model moves although they stay still
+ first lawan object with no cause (not acted on, not moving, not airborne, not touched or about to be hit) is held exactly, also when predicting
+ third law at impactsat the moment two moving objects collide their momentum changes cancel (weight 0.2), only where the physics engine itself satisfies it
+ chained blocksafter 40% of training, up to half of the training blocks start from the model's own prediction of the block before, so it learns to recover from its own errors
+ fast-spin data4,415 more training simulations: each object of the four scenes spun at 10-50 rad/s
+ statics + rollingan object at rest lies on a face of its box (weight 0.5); round objects roll without slipping (weight 0.2)

The laws are each added alone on top of gravity + velocity from motion (regression). Every model: 8,000 training steps from the MOVi + Physion pretrained weights, the same as stage 1.

Takeaways

  • Built-in gravity, now applied at the centre of mass (the first version used the object's base; its error against the physics engine on free flights fell from 0.72 m to 6 mm), keeps stage 1's position error (regression 0.242 vs 0.241 m, flow matching 0.330 vs 0.324 m) with slightly worse tilt (20.5 vs 18.3 degrees, regression). It is the starting point of the V3 and V5 retraining.
  • Velocity from motion lowers tilt error (17.6 degrees) and raises toppling precision (82%) but raises position error by 11% (0.269 m) and lowers toppling recall (57%): dropped.
  • Of the laws, only two help their target. Relative overlap lowers relative passing through by 28% (0.32 -> 0.23; 1.9 -> 1.6 cm) at 12% more position error. Counting objects moving in truth or prediction is the only addition that lowers position error (0.269 -> 0.262 m) and passing through (0.32 -> 0.30). The first law is neutral. The third law at impacts and chained blocks make every measure worse (position error +18% and +26%; the third law drops toppling recall to 34%).
  • Fast-spin data: on held-out fast spins position error falls by 19% (regression 0.212 -> 0.172 m) and 21% (flow matching 0.287 -> 0.228 m), rotation error 81 -> 69 degrees, toppling precision 84% -> 90%; on the original held-out actions it costs 6% (regression) and 3% (flow matching).
  • Statics + rolling: impossible resting poses do not fall (13% -> 14% on spins), and position error on held-out actions rises (regression 0.284 -> 0.349 m): not kept at these weights.
  • Clips: with fast-spin data the spun barrel travels to the wall and, after a fast spin, falls and rolls toward the camera as in the physics engine, where stage 1's best regression keeps it standing. Still wrong in every model: the barrel should end upright against the wall, and the bowling-pin throw knocks the bucket over only in stage 1's best flow-matching model (every stage-2 variant leaves it standing).
  • Next: V3 and V5 are retrained on all data with built-in gravity, from this stage's weights. Relative overlap and moving-in-truth-or-prediction are candidates at lower weight; the third law, chained blocks, statics and rolling stay out until a version improves its target without costing position error.

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
faulty in every table so far (found 16 Oct 2026): it counted objects resting on the floor, or on anything whose contact the model missed, as in free flight, so every model and the physics engine itself score about 8-10. Ignore this column; the free-fall error replaces it.
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
Passes through, relative
the same overlap divided by the thinnest side of the object that passes (a 2 cm overlap is small for a table but goes through a tray); average over simulations.
Position error, moving in truth or prediction
average position error over the objects that move in the physics engine or in the prediction, so objects the model moves wrongly also count (metres).
Rotation error
average angle between the predicted and true orientation of moving objects other than balls, over the 3 s (degrees).
Impossible resting pose
of the objects that end at rest lying flat on a face in the physics engine, the share the model leaves at rest on an edge or corner (no box side within 18 degrees of the floor).
Airborne error
position error while an object is in the air in the physics engine, touching nothing (metres).
Free-fall error
how far the predicted vertical acceleration, measured over 0.1 s, is from gravity on stretches where the physics engine's own motion is a free fall (its vertical acceleration within 0.5 m/s² of gravity for 7 frames in a row); m/s². The physics engine itself scores 0.02, and 2 mm of position jitter adds about 0.4.
