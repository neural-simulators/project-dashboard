Improved Results V4

Iteration 4: which change helps rotation (toppling), position error and passing through most, tested first by over-fitting four training scenes.

Ground truth is the physics engine (PhysX in NVIDIA Isaac Sim). A learned simulator predicts how every object moves for 3 s after an action, without running the engine. Every model here is trained on the same data and tested on the same 418 frozen tasks and the same simulations as the Scaled results tab, so the numbers compare directly.

What changed (15 Oct 2026, overfitting stage evaluated, clips rendered)↑ contents

Every model so far has a tilt error of about 27 degrees on the test set and catches only about half of the real topples. Before training on all the data, each rotation change is tested where the model should be able to fit: four training scenes (a brick slid toward a wall, with 1,132 wall hits; a bucket, pin, crate and golf ball; a skittle knocked over; a domino chain), about 19,300 simulations made for this (many actions per scene, each action also with one parameter varied: speed, direction, height, contact point). 80% of the simulations of each scene train the model; 20% are held out (actions not seen in that scene). Base for every variant: step-wise + box geometry + no-penetration loss, with hold still counting a turn of more than 2 degrees as moving.

VariantWhat it adds
baseV3's no-penetration model; hold still now counts turning as moving (a topple starts with little centre motion)
+ rotation as a changerotation predicted as the change from the block start, scaled like positions
+ angle lossthe angle between predicted and true orientation as its own loss (weight 20)
+ orientation from spinorientation integrated from the predicted spin over the block
+ symmetric axis + topple weightfor objects symmetric about their axis only the axis is supervised, balls not at all; triple weight on blocks where an object tips by more than 10 degrees; with the angle loss
corners as outputsthe network outputs each object's 8 box corners; position and orientation follow by a rigid fit (keypoints instead of rotation numbers, as in pose estimation)
change + angle + symmetric + topplesall but spin and corners together
base on 50% / 25% of the datalearning curve: does more data of the same scenes help?

Every variant: 8,000 training steps from the MOVi + Physion pretrained weights, regression and flow matching.

Takeaways↑ contents

  • Corners as outputs gives the largest gain: the only change that lowers position error for both objectives (regression 0.263 -> 0.241 m, flow matching 0.338 -> 0.327 m on held-out actions), and it roughly doubles toppling recall (regression 41% -> 67%, flow matching 32% -> 74%), with lower precision (82% -> 75%, 80% -> 65%); passing through rises by about 0.5 cm, which guidance can take back.
  • In the clips the gain of corners as outputs is visible where toppling matters: when a thrown bowling pin knocks the bucket over, only corners as outputs tips the bucket onto its side as the physics engine does; every other variant leaves it standing. On the barrel spun into the wall no variant is right; the variants with the angle loss scatter or freeze pieces in the skittle and domino scenes.
  • Rotation as a change from the block start also raises toppling recall (to 62-63%) and slightly lowers tilt error, but costs position error (+11% regression, +7% flow matching). Orientation from spin helps regression's position (0.249 m) but has the worst tilt error.
  • The angle loss gives the lowest tilt error (16.9 degrees, regression) but at weight 20 it drowns the position terms (0.389 m); with the symmetric-axis loss and in the combination the same happens: the weight needs to be much smaller.
  • No variant fits even its training actions: errors on training and held-out actions are the same (about 0.24-0.34 m), and 25% of the data is almost as good as all of it. The loss on single 0.2 s blocks is small; the error comes from chaining 15 blocks, each starting from the model's own previous prediction, and grows fastest around collisions. The next step is to train on several chained blocks at once (the model's own predictions as starts, as in GNS's pushforward and self-forcing).

How to read the tables↑ contents

Measures
Average position error
average distance between predicted and true positions of moving objects over the 3 s (metres; lower is better).
Same verdict
share of test simulations where the task verdict computed from the predicted motion equals the physics engine's.
Bystanders moved
objects that the physics engine keeps still (they move less than 1 cm) but the learned simulator moves by more than 5 cm.
Moved before touched
objects not acted on that the learned simulator moves (more than 2 cm) while in reality they have not started moving yet.
Overlap beyond the real one
how much deeper predicted objects overlap each other than in the real motion (worst moment, metres).
Joint stretch
how much a pendulum arm, rope link or other rigid joint changes length in the prediction (metres; 0 in reality).
Back-and-forth
times per moving object that the predicted motion reverses direction while nothing is touching it.
Free-flight error
difference between the predicted acceleration of an object in free flight and gravity (m/s²; 0 in reality).
Energy from nowhere
largest gain in total energy beyond what the action put in (joules; 0 in reality).
Tilt error
average difference between the predicted and true tilt of moving objects other than balls (angle of the up axis from vertical, degrees, over the 3 s); ignores spin about the vertical.
Toppling recall / precision
of the objects upright after the action, those that end tilted more than 45 degrees: the share of real topples the model also predicts (recall) and the share of predicted topples that really happen (precision).
Passes through
per simulation, the largest amount by which a predicted pair of objects overlaps more deeply than the same pair in the physics engine (oriented boxes of the objects' pieces, 3 checks per frame, so passing through a thin wall between frames counts; the floor excluded). Shown as the average in cm and the share of simulations above 5 cm.
